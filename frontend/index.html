    📚 10 Golden Rules of Password & Identity Hygiene

    Security Awareness Guide
    01

    Length Beats Complexity

    A 16-character passphrase composed of 4 random words is far harder to crack than an 8-character word with forced symbols (e.g. P@ssw0rd!).

    02

    Never Reuse Passwords Across Accounts

    Credential stuffing bots automatically replay leaked passwords across thousands of services. A single leak compromises every reused account.

    03

    Avoid Predictable Personal Info

    Adversaries scrape OSINT (social media, public records) to guess birth years, spouse/children names, pet names, and hometowns.

    04

    Eliminate Keyboard Walks & Sequences

    Patterns like qwerty, 123456, and 1qaz2wsx are the first entries tested by automated password-cracking dictionaries.

    05

    Adopt a Trusted Password Manager

    Tools like Bitwarden or 1Password generate unique 20+ character passwords for every website, removing memory burden and defeating phishing duplicates.

    06

    Mandate Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA)

    Even if an attacker discovers your password, hardware keys (FIDO2/WebAuthn) or authenticator apps (TOTP) stop 99% of automated credential takeover attempts.

    07

    Reject Forced Periodic Expirations

    NIST SP 800-63B advises against mandatory 90-day password changes because users react by making predictable incremental edits (e.g. Spring2025! → Summer2025!).

    08

    Never Transmit Plaintext Credentials

    Never share passwords over email, Slack, chat, or unencrypted HTTP channels. Use secure one-time secret-sharing links when necessary.

    09

    Stay Vigilant Against Social Engineering & Phishing

    The strongest password cannot protect you if you type it into a fraudulent replica login page. Always inspect the browser URL bar and certificate.

    10

    Rotate Immediately Upon Breach Notification

    Subscribe to reputable breach monitoring services (e.g. HaveIBeenPwned) and rotate credentials immediately if a service you use suffers a leak.
